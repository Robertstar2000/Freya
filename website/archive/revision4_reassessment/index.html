SCIENCE BEFORE HARDWARE FREEZE

Sample depth is a science decision

Shallow skin

Investigate roughly 0–3 cm material influenced by the daily thermal wave. Warmth does not guarantee liquid water, freeze–thaw cycles or active biology. Define the surface sampler.

Buried measurements

Burial depth and sampled depth are different. Moving shallow material to a buried instrument needs a transport mechanism. The current sampler allocation does not prove this capability.

Controlled assays

Define detection limits, blanks, abiotic controls, replication, incubation and operating temperatures. Heating changes the sample. No universal revival time is assumed.

Supplement [13]: a 45 g humidity sensor occupies 124 cm³. That is about 80% of the reduced science envelope. Small mass does not guarantee fit. Instrument readiness labels are not FREYA qualification.

Thermal-wave attenuation

Homogeneous half-space only. Layering, ice and local conditions can change the answer. Broad annual means are about −55°C equator to −119°C poles [3].

Evidence and open decisions

InSight provides a low-conductivity thermal reference [2]. The CAPyR reanalysis [5] is a submitted preprint, not a confirmed life detection. IMPRESS architecture and dimensions come from the attached published paper [1].

Neither payload size has a supplied instrument CAD or demonstrated full metabolic experiment. A compact sensor may fit; equal scientific capability is not established.

Contamination treatment

Whole-probe irradiation is not mandatory. Gas-filled glove-box assembly and ethylene oxide are candidates. Historical NASA evidence [4] requires compatibility and residue evaluation. Mission classification and microbial endpoints remain open.
