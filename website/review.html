Penetration and effective sampling depth

The penetration study and the updated material comparison are both incorporated in full later in this report. The active website permits the 0.75 m stopping-distance scenario while keeping depth achievement unproven. It distinguishes whole-probe 4 kg impact arithmetic from the nominal forebody mass that actually penetrates in the split architecture.

For axial nose travel s, tip-to-inlet offset o, tilt theta from vertical and an excluded disturbed-layer allowance c, effective vertical inlet depth is z=(s-o) cos(theta)-c. To reach 0.50 m, s_required=o+(0.50+c)/cos(theta). This assumes a straight penetrator and planar original surface. Crater geometry and the disturbed zone require measurement, not a fixed universal correction.

At zero disturbance and zero tilt, 0.75-0.25=0.50 m. With a 0.05 m excluded layer, even vertical travel must reach 0.80 m. At 30 degrees, 0.75 m axial travel leaves only 0.383 m effective depth for that allowance. These are geometric sensitivities, not crater predictions.

At 4 kg, 150 m/s and an assumed 0.75 m stopping distance, energy is 45 kJ, average deceleration 1,529.6 Earth g and mean force 60 kN. Peak shock and local science-mount stress remain unknown. The 100-250 m/s velocity table in the material annex is preserved; none of its rows solves sandstone penetration.

Revision 6 retains Young/Sandia relative scaling x proportional to (m/A)^0.7 (v-30.5), holding target, nose and mass correction equal. The primary report is C W Young, Penetration Equations, SAND97-2426 (1997), DOI 10.2172/562498 [N6]. It is empirical terrestrial heritage; a Mars target calibration is still absent. It cannot validate the hard-sandstone requirement. Carbon fiber versus steel architecture

The updated 0.75 m material study recommends tempered alloy steel for the nose and primary compression load path; CFRP remains an option for rear or secondary structure pending tests. Revision 6 extends that proposal into a steel-head/CFRP-barrel comparison. Both positions are retained. The numerical hybrid screen below does not supersede the conservative steel test article recommendation.

The reproduced hybrid replaces the final 20 mm of composite barrel with a steel collar whose radial wall is twice the nominal wall. It retains the steel nose, cap and 0.120 kg joint allowance. Density is 1,580 kg/m3 for CFRP and 7,850 kg/m3 for steel. These geometry and material assumptions reproduce the Revision 6 structural masses to printed precision.

The collar intrudes into the nominal bore by about 0.00982 L for 70/2.5. Therefore equal cavity fit is not automatic. CAD must show the collar location, loss of usable volume, adhesive and fastener volumes, load transfer and cold mismatch. The current mass table is the reproduced screen before that packaging correction.

The 70 mm hybrid reduces nominal forebody mass from 3.520 to 2.708 kg. Relative penetration scaling is (2.708/3.520)^0.7=0.832, a 16.8% reduction under the stated equal-factor model. The sectional-density reduction itself is 23.1%, not 17%. Revision 6 section 5.9 uses 17% in one prose statement about sectional density; its table and exponent distinguish the two quantities correctly.

A generic 40-60% residual compressive-strength reduction is not a design allowable for an unspecified laminate. Select grade, resin, layup, fiber direction, joint, temperature and impact damage state. Measure cold compression-after-impact and joint response. Static Euler margins do not address local crushing, delamination, fracture, bending, nose erosion, seals or electronic shock.

Packaging thermal and power closure

The full IMPRESS paper is now locally available. Section 3.1, printed page 691, identifies a 50 mm OD, 1.5 mm wall and 210 mm cylinder. It describes 345 cm3 internal capacity, including 40 cm3 for core telemetry, battery and power regulation, leaving 305 cm3 theoretical science volume. The test article substitutes epoxy for science. The approximately 2 kg Mars-ready probe is a proposed platform, not evidence that the FREYA 1 kg assay suite fits.

The paper describes planned tests at 115 m/s and up to 5,200 g; those future-tense passages are not test results. Its 10,000 g accelerometer rating is a sensor range, not a certified whole-probe survival rating. The article attributes development support to NASA TechLeap and CHiwi; that is distinct from the user-reported Stone Aerospace SBIR proposal.

The 0.3085 kg reduced payload ceiling is a FREYA inverse budget. It combines 0.345 L practical cavity, 25% potting, 10% free space, 0.070 L ancillary volume and 2 kg/L science density. It is not the published IMPRESS science mass. The supplementary 45 g / 124 cm3 humidity sensor occupies about 80.4% of this reduced envelope. S1 is mission heritage, S2 instrument candidates, S3 broader astrobiology concepts; these tables do not supply a jointly qualified suite.

All 16 nominal hardware lines remain in the live mass ledger. The standard 0.120 L ancillary envelope remains an optimistic legacy input, visibly labeled. A 0.45 kg D-cell analogy requires about 0.243 L before controllers and insulation. Integer cells, chemistry, rate, packaging and impact protection must replace the fractional-cell analogy.

At 70/2.5, changing science density to 1 kg/L and ancillary volume to 0.35 L gives 7.634 kg and 686 mm. This is a stress scenario, not a probabilistic bound. Battery mass changes do not automatically allocate volume; the site warns about that uncoupled input.

For radial series conduction, Rsoil=ln(r_far/r_outer)/(2 pi k_soil L), Rshell=ln(r_outer/r_inner)/(2 pi k_shell L), and Q=DeltaT/(Rsoil+Rshell). With ksoil=0.039 W/m K, rfar=0.5 m and DeltaT=50 K, the 70 mm steel case loses 1.32436 W and the CFRP case 1.32259 W through the barrel-only path. Shell conductivity changes by roughly 56 times, but total loss changes only about 0.13% in this soil-limited scenario. Endcaps, tether, radiation, contact, transient effects and insulation are excluded.

Electrical energy closure is E=sum(P_i times duty_i times duration)+startup, with battery mass E/(usable Wh/kg). The legacy 1.5 W and 5 Wh startup assumptions give 41.7 h with 0.45 kg at 150 Wh/kg, or 185 Wh / 1.233 kg for 120 h. The warmed 130 Wh profile in Revision 6 is a different assumed duty cycle, not interchangeable with 185 Wh. Avoid double-counting heater loss already included in an average electrical load. No biological response duration is guaranteed.

•
The mass and volume model is sound. An independent re-implementation reproduced all six candidate masses, the 70
mm ledger, the 2 kg reverse budget, the battery table, the orbit numbers and the thermal numbers to the printed

•
Diameter mostly trades length, strength, penetration and heat loss, not mass. At 1 kg of science and equal cavity
volume the complete probe budget is 5.01–5.73 kg across 35–80 mm. The shortlist from Revision 4 stands, now derived
rather than asserted: 35/1.5 is rejected on a buckling screen (Euler margin 0.87 at average load); 60/2 and 70/2.5 are
the primary candidates (top three in all five weighting sets); 50/1.5 is kept as the heritage comparator.
•
The largest uncertainty is packaging, not structure. Two unsourced inputs dominate length and mass: science bulk
density (2.0 kg/L assumed; the only supplement datum is 0.36 kg/L) and ancillary volume (0.120 L assumed; the battery
cells alone need 0.243 L). Together they could move the 70 mm case from 347 mm and 5.49 kg to about 686 mm and
7.63 kg (PR-31, PR-32).
•
The largest feasibility gap is energy–thermal–science compatibility. The 70 mm barrel loses 1.3 W to the soil at ΔT = 50
K, nearly all of the 1.5 W profile that gives the 41.7 h runtime. Measured microbial doubling times near −10 to −20 °C
are 20–160 days. The baseline battery supports brief sensing or a short, insulated, warmed assay, not delayed-recovery
biology (PR-04, PR-05).
•
Delivery is quantified. From a 250 km orbit, 61.6 m/s gives an entry angle near −2.0° at 3.49 km/s; −13° needs about
889 m/s. Only the entry stack needs deorbit propulsion (1.24 kg for 45 kg versus 12.4 kg for the 450 kg parent). Six
probes do not close in 45 kg unless non-probe mass is at most 26.8 % of entry mass; at the DS2 aeroshell fraction (32.6
%) five probes close (PR-13, PR-14).
•
Communications can close at UHF, but the radio and the relay network are open items. An illustrative budget shows
about +4 dB margin at 8 kbps and 1 W to a relay orbiter at 1,000 km. The AX100 analog is not a Proximity-1 radio, and
MAVEN has ended (PR-10, PR-11).
•
Schedule is the planning constraint. A December 2028 launch is about 26 months away. A life-detection payload with
planetary-protection validation is not credible on that timeline; a reduced pathfinder might be (planning judgment,
Section 13).

3 Mission context and study basis
3.1 What the study supports, and how objectives trace to requirements
FREYA is a penetrator-class probe concept carrying a 1.000 kg science package into the Martian shallow subsurface, with a
buried forebody, a tether and a surface aftbody carrying the radio. It follows the IMPRESS architecture, which emplaces
instruments 0.2–1 m below the surface and operates probes as a network [1]. This study supports a feasibility decision and
early mission planning. It does not select a flight design.

The mass model has no structural check. The Euler screen in Section 5.3 gives each candidate a column-buckling margin
against the average axial impact load. It converts the rejection of 35/1.5 into a reproducible result (PR-16). It does not
account for soil lateral support, which helps, nor for bending from oblique impact, local wall buckling, nose design, joint
strength or cold embrittlement, which hurt. DS2 expected an angle of attack below 12° [20]; FREYA has no dispersion analysis.
The structural question is therefore reduced, not closed: finite-element analysis and cold oblique impact tests (R05, R06)
remain the gating evidence, and hardness, toughness and joint design must be chosen together (MECH01).

12.3 Special Region screening and the warmed assay
A Special Region is a region within which terrestrial organisms are likely to replicate, or which is interpreted to have a high
potential for extant Martian life. The working thresholds are a temperature of at least 255 K (−18 °C) and a water activity
above 0.60 (the SR-SAG2 findings as summarized in [33]); NASA and NASEM carry the more conservative −28 °C (245 K) and
0.5 [32]. The shallow soil at InSight averages 217.5 K [2], well below the threshold, but thin brine films can occur there [2]. A
warmed assay chamber at +5 to +20 °C would exceed the temperature threshold; whether it also exceeds the water-activity
threshold depends on sample humidity and salts, and whether the mission authority treats a spacecraft-created warm, wet
volume as a Special Region is the authority's decision. NRC language describes Category IVc missions as those going to a place
where liquid water is present or where the presence of the spacecraft could cause it to be present [33].
Design response: record the maximum temperature, humidity and time above threshold for every wetted or humid volume
(PP04), and ask the mission authority early whether Mode C triggers IVc (decision D-01).

Core model (Python, as used for the review)
import math
RS, RE = 7850.0, 1100.0                       # steel and potting density, kg/m3
def design(D, t, sci=1.0, rho_sci=2.0, anc=0.120, pot=0.25, res=0.10, batt=0.450, cont=0.25,
           heater=0.120, harn=0.080, samp=0.150, xcvr=0.100, ant=0.060, cable=0.050,
           spool=0.060, aft=0.200, drogue=0.250, joints=0.120):
    d = D - 2*t
    V = (sci/rho_sci + anc) / (1 - pot - res)                  # litres
    L = (V/1000) / (math.pi*d**2/4)                            # barrel length, m
    tube = RS*math.pi*(D**2 - d**2)*L/4
    nose = RS*(2*math.pi/3)*((D/2)**3 - (d/2)**3)
    cap  = RS*math.pi*D**2*0.005/4
    steel = tube + nose + cap + joints
    epoxy = RE*pot*V/1000
    fore = sci + steel + epoxy + batt + heater + harn
    aftb = samp + xcvr + ant + cable + spool + aft + drogue
    nom = fore + aftb; env = L + D/2 + 0.005 + 0.020
    return dict(V=V, L=L, steel=steel, fore=fore, nom=nom, tot=nom*(1+cont), env=env)
