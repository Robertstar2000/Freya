Mission abstract

FREYA is a Mars surface-science mission that delivers independent penetrator probes from orbit. The late-2028 precursor concept uses one 20 kg vehicle with three probes. The 2031 concept uses two 50 kg vehicles with seven probes each. From a 400 km circular reference orbit, each retro module separates from the host, targets and executes a deorbit maneuver, aligns the heat shield, and jettisons before entry. The capsule protects the carrier during passive entry. The carrier is extracted under parachute, releases probes one at a time, and reports deployment status independently. Each probe descends under its own drogue, acquires a traceable sample after impact, and queues science and health records for relay through a Mars orbiter. The science package evaluates candidate assays and controls for interpretable measurements in the Martian near-surface environment.

Mission elements

Aeroshell

The aeroshell is the entry capsule assembly of forebody heat shield, backshell, restraints and top cover. It supports passive drag deceleration and protects the stowed carrier until extraction. The reference is a 0.800 m diameter sphere-cone with a 70° half-angle. The shell and cover are discarded as the carrier begins independent descent.

Retro module

The detachable reentry module contains retro propulsion, nitrogen attitude and separation jets, guidance, power and release hardware. It performs the retroburn and establishes heat-shield-forward attitude before transferring state to the retained avionics and separating before the 125 km reference entry interface.

Carrier

The carrier holds the probe bays, sequencing hardware, parachute, avionics, and independent power and status radio. It is extracted at a 10 km reference altitude, then releases probes sequentially beginning at a 5 km reference and a 2 s nominal cadence. The reference canopies are 1.5 m on A and 2.0 m on B.

Probe

Each approximately 2 kg probe has a 0.50 m drogue, impact-resistant body, sample intake, science chamber, sensors, storage, and separate antenna and battery. It records sampling location, time, depth and environmental conditions, and transmits buffered records during relay contacts. Nominal surface-operation goals are 24 h on A and 72 h on B.
